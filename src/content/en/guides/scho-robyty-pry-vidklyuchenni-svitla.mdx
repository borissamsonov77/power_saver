A power outage is stressful, especially if it happens unexpectedly or will last for hours. This guide helps you act calmly and correctly.

## First 5 Minutes: Safety Check

**Unplug large appliances.** This protects them from voltage surges when power is restored:
- Leave the refrigerator and freezer plugged in; unplug everything else
- Unplug the stove, water heater, washing machine, and air conditioner

**Check your breaker panel.** If neighbors have power but you don't, a tripped circuit breaker may be the cause. Find your panel (usually in a hallway or utility area) and check that all breakers are in the ON position.

<SafetyWarning message="⚠️ Do not touch wires or attempt to repair electrical wiring yourself. If you smell burning or see sparks, evacuate immediately and call emergency services." />

## Find Out the Cause and Duration

**Your utility provider's app or website** is the fastest way to check for scheduled or emergency outages. Search for your provider's official Telegram channel, social media account, or outage map.

**Save your utility's emergency number** in your phone. In Ukraine: 104 (from mobile). In the EU: check your local Netzbetreiber or DSO website.

## During the First 1-2 Hours

### Charge Your Phone
If you have a powerbank, start charging your phone immediately. Your phone is your primary connection to information and emergency services.

**How much do you need:** A full charge plus one backup cycle. A 10,000 mAh powerbank gives 2-3 full charges for an average smartphone.

### Get Lighting
**Priority: flashlight or headlamp.** Avoid candles — they are a fire hazard, especially around children or pets.

If candles are your only option: never leave them unattended, keep them away from curtains and paper.

### Keep the Refrigerator Closed
- Closed refrigerator stays at safe temperature **for up to 4 hours**
- Closed freezer stays safe **for up to 48 hours**
- For extended outages: move fridge items into the freezer

## Staying Warm in Winter

If it's winter and you have radiators or heated flooring:
1. Gather everyone in one room — it's easier to keep a smaller space warm
2. Put on warm clothes before you get cold, not after
3. Do not use gas stoves or cooking burners for heating — risk of carbon monoxide poisoning

<SafetyWarning message="⚠️ Never use gas stoves, grills, camp stoves, or generators INDOORS for heating or lighting. Risk of fatal carbon monoxide (CO) poisoning." />

## Preparing for an Extended Outage (8+ hours)

If you learn the outage will be long:

1. **Fill containers with water** — pump stations may stop if the outage is widespread
2. **Charge all devices** — while powerbanks and power stations still have power
3. **Cook food that requires heating** — while gas is still available
4. **Notify family and friends** — let them know where you are and how to reach you

## Checklist for Next Time

This situation is your wake-up call to build a minimal autonomy kit:
- [ ] Full powerbank (always kept charged)
- [ ] Rechargeable headlamp
- [ ] Water reserve (3 liters per person per day)
- [ ] Paper list of important phone numbers
