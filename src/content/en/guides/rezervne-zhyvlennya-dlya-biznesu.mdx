Power outages for a business aren't just inconvenient — they mean lost revenue, spoiled inventory, and frustrated customers. Let's walk through how to choose backup power for your specific type of business.

## Step 1: Identify Your Critical Load

Before choosing equipment, you need to answer: **what absolutely must keep running** during an outage?

### Typical Critical Systems:

| System | Wattage | Priority |
|--------|---------|----------|
| POS terminal and register | 50–100W | Critical |
| Router and Wi-Fi | 10–20W | Critical |
| Server equipment | 200–500W | Critical |
| Refrigerators (food/product) | 100–300W × count | Critical |
| Minimum lighting | 100–300W | High |
| Coffee machine / bar equipment | 500–1500W | Medium |

**Practical approach:** List your appliances and mark which ones cannot be turned off. Calculate the total wattage of everything on the "cannot turn off" list.

## Step 2: Assess Expected Outage Duration

- **Up to 2 hours:** UPS can cover critical load
- **2-8 hours:** Power station or generator needed
- **8+ hours:** Generator or industrial energy storage required

## Solutions by Business Type

### Café or Bar

**Minimal level (under $400):**
- UPS 1,500 VA for POS + router: 2-4 hours of cash register operation
- 500 Wh power station for main lighting

**Optimal level ($800–$1,500):**
- 2,000 Wh power station (EcoFlow DELTA Pro or similar)
- Covers: register, router, lighting, small drinks fridge — 6-8 hours

### Retail Store or Supermarket

**Key challenge:** Freezer cases and refrigerators are the main load. Generator required.

- **5-10 kW generator** + ATS (Automatic Transfer Switch)
- UPS for cash registers and IT systems — to cover the generator startup gap (2-3 minutes)

<SafetyWarning message="⚠️ Connecting a generator to a building's electrical system ALWAYS requires a licensed electrician via an Automatic Transfer Switch (ATS). Parallel connection to the grid without an ATS is life-threatening and may be illegal under local codes." />

### Office

**Priority:** Server equipment, PCs, VoIP phones.

- **Server-grade UPS** with minimal transfer time (0 ms or &lt;10 ms)
- Power station for charging laptops and mobile devices
- Backup Starlink or 4G router for internet continuity

## Safety and Regulatory Notes

### What regulations typically require:

1. **Generators must be installed outdoors** or in specially ventilated enclosures — check local fire and building codes
2. **ATS is required** when connecting to a building's electrical system
3. **Diesel generators** in multi-tenant buildings may require fire safety permits
4. **Fuel storage** is regulated by local fire safety standards

<SafetyWarning message="⚠️ Never run gasoline or diesel generators indoors, in basements, or in garages. Fatal carbon monoxide (CO) poisoning risk." />

## Small Business Checklist

- [ ] Identified critical load (list of appliances and wattages)
- [ ] Determined maximum acceptable downtime
- [ ] Consulted a licensed electrician about connection requirements
- [ ] Checked local permit requirements for generator installation
- [ ] Created a power failure procedure for staff
